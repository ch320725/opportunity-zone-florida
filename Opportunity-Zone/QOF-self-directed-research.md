## 1) Legal structure
- A QOF is any investment vehicle organized as a **corporation or a partnership** (an LLC taxed as either counts) for the purpose of investing in QOZ property. It must hold at least 90% of its assets in QOZP (§1400Z-2(d)(1)). Most real-estate QOFs are LLCs taxed as partnerships.
- **Self-certification:** No IRS approval or application is required. The entity certifies itself by filing **Form 8996** with its timely filed federal return (Form 1065 or 1120), including extensions. On that first return it picks the first month it is a QOF. The same form is used every year for the 90% test (Form 8996 instructions).
- The entity has to be created or organized in one of the 50 states, DC, or a US possession. Its governing documents should state the purpose of investing in QOZP. (Regs §1.1400Z2(d)-1; Form 8996 instructions.)
- **Two-tier option:** The fund can hold QOZ business property directly ("one-tier"). It can also own stock or partnership interests in a QOZ business (QOZB) subsidiary ("two-tier"). Two-tier is common because the QOZB gets the 70% test and the working-capital safe harbor, which a direct-holding QOF does not.

## 2) Requirements
- **90% asset test (QOF level):** QOZP must be at least 90% of assets, averaged over two testing dates: the last day of the first 6-month period of the tax year and the last day of the tax year. Measurement uses audited financial-statement values or cost basis (Regs §1.1400Z2(d)-1). Under the regs, the fund can exclude newly contributed cash or property received in the 6 months before a testing date, provided it was held in cash, cash equivalents or short-term debt.
- **QOZP** means QOZ stock, a QOZ partnership interest, or QOZ business property (QOZBP).
- **QOZBP:** Tangible property used in a trade or business in a QOZ. It must be acquired by purchase (as defined in §179(d)(2)) after 12/31/2017 from an unrelated party. It must meet either the original-use test or the substantial-improvement test, and substantially all of its use must be in a QOZ during substantially all of the holding period (§1400Z-2(d)(2)(D)).
  - **Original use:** The property's original use in the QOZ starts with the fund or QOZB. Under the regs, vacant property (vacant at least 1 year before the zone designation, or 3 years) can qualify as original use.
  - **Substantial improvement:** Within any 30-month period after acquisition, additions to basis must be more than the adjusted basis at the start of that period, which roughly doubles the basis. For a building, **land is excluded**: only the building basis has to be doubled. Land itself does not have to be improved, but it must be used in a trade or business, and anti-abuse rules apply to land banking.
- **QOZ business tests (two-tier, QOZB level), at a high level:**
  - "Substantially all" of tangible property (regs: **at least 70%**) is QOZBP.
  - At least 50% of gross income comes from the active conduct of business in the QOZ. The regs offer hours, services and tangible-property safe harbors.
  - A substantial portion of intangibles is used in the business (regs: 40%).
  - Nonqualified financial property is under 5% of assets, unless covered by the working-capital safe harbor.
  - No "sin businesses" (§144(c)(6)(B)): golf course, country club, massage parlor, hot tub or suntan facility, racetrack or gambling facility, or liquor store.
  - Triple-net leasing alone is not an active trade or business for the regs' purposes. Rental real estate generally needs meaningful management.
- **Working-capital safe harbor (QOZB only):** Cash can be held up to **31 months** if three things are true: a written plan designates it for acquisition, construction or improvement of property; there is a written schedule; and the cash is actually spent substantially as planned. The regs allow overlapping or sequential plans of up to 62 months, plus extra time for government permitting delays and federally declared disasters.
- **Reinvestment:** A QOF has 12 months to reinvest proceeds from the sale of QOZP or a return of capital and still count them as qualifying (Regs §1.1400Z2(f)-1).

## 3) Investor side
- **Eligible gain:** Capital gain, including §1231 gain (net §1231 under the regs), from a sale to an unrelated person. The investor must invest an amount up to that gain in a QOF, in exchange for an equity interest, **within 180 days** of the sale. Partners in a pass-through can also start the 180 days at the end of the entity's tax year or at its return due date. The investor elects deferral on **Form 8949** and files **Form 8997** every year.
- **Deferral for investments made through 12/31/2026 (legacy rules):** The deferred gain is included on the earlier of sale of the QOF interest or **December 31, 2026**. The included amount is the lesser of the deferred gain or FMV, minus basis. For most legacy investors the tax is due with the 2026 return, filed in 2027.
- **5- and 7-year basis step-ups (10% and 15%), legacy rules:** These are effectively sunset. To get the 10% step-up an investor needed 5 years of holding by 12/31/2026, so investment by 12/31/2021. The extra 5% at 7 years required investment by 12/31/2019. A new investment made in 2026 gets neither step-up.
- **10-year exclusion:** If the QOF interest is held 10 or more years, the investor can elect basis equal to FMV at sale (§1400Z-2(c)). That makes the post-investment appreciation tax-free. The deferred original gain is still taxed. For legacy zones the regs allow this election through 12/31/2047.
- **OZ 2.0 (OBBBA §70421) for investments made after 12/31/2026:**
  - The program is permanent.
  - There is a rolling deferral: gain is recognized on the earlier of sale or **5 years after the investment**.
  - Investments held 5 years get a **10% basis step-up**, or **30%** for a "qualified rural opportunity fund."
  - The 10-year FMV exclusion remains, but appreciation is capped by freezing FMV at the **30-year** mark.
  - The OBBBA also added QOF reporting and penalty provisions. Confirm the effective dates and final forms with your CPA.

## 4) Practical steps and rough costs
**Steps:**
1. Find the gain and its 180-day window. Confirm it is capital or §1231 gain.
2. Pick the property or parcel and confirm it lies in a designated QOZ tract (Treasury/CDFI list; Florida Commerce map). Remember that legacy designations end 12/31/2026 (see section 5).
3. Choose the structure, one-tier or two-tier (QOF LLC owning a QOZB LLC).
4. Form the LLC(s) in Florida through Sunbiz, get an EIN, and draft an operating agreement with the QOF purpose. Elect partnership tax treatment, which needs 2 or more members, or corporate treatment.
5. Contribute the gain cash and receive equity. Keep proof of the date.
6. Write and document the working-capital plan and schedule at the QOZB, and track the 30-month substantial-improvement spend.
7. File Form 8996 with the QOF's first return to self-certify, then file it every year for the 90% test. The investor files Form 8949 for the deferral election and Form 8997 every year.
8. Keep testing-date valuations and business-activity records for the full hold of 10 years or more.

**Can a single investor form their own fund?** Yes. Nothing in the statute or regs requires multiple investors, and "self-directed" single-investor QOFs are common. There is one catch: a **single-member LLC is disregarded** by default, so it is not a partnership and cannot be a partnership QOF. A solo investor can handle this in either of two ways:
- add a second member, such as a spouse (outside community-property rules) or a related entity, so the LLC is taxed as a partnership; or
- elect corporate (C-corp) treatment on Form 8832. This is less common because of the double tax.

The IRS FAQs confirm that a QOF must be a partnership or a corporation for federal tax purposes.

**Rough costs:** No IRS or state certification fee is charged for QOF status; Form 8996 is free to file. The estimates below are informal market ranges, not from a primary source. Get quotes.
- Florida LLC filing: $125 per LLC (Sunbiz articles of organization plus registered agent designation), plus an annual report of about $138.75. See https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/ and verify current fees there.
- Attorney for formation and OZ operating agreements: roughly $3k–$15k for a simple single-investor setup, more for a syndicated fund.
- CPA for annual partnership returns plus Forms 8996 and 8997: roughly $2k–$7k per year per entity.
- Valuation or audit for the 90% testing: varies.
- Optional turnkey self-directed QOF providers charge setup plus annual fees.

## 5) Florida / Lee County
- **Florida Commerce** (formerly DEO) administered the governor's 2018 nominations: 427 Florida tracts were designated. See https://www.floridajobs.org/ (Opportunity Zones page).
- **Lee County has 15 designated tracts** under the 2018 round, per the project's oz-tracts data in this repo.
- **State tax overlay:** Florida has **no personal income tax**, so individual investors care only about the federal benefit. The Florida corporate income tax (Ch. 220) starts from federal taxable income, so the federal OZ treatment generally flows through for C-corps. Confirm current conformity with your CPA.
- No Florida-specific QOF registration or certification exists. Local incentives such as Lee County economic development programs may stack with OZ benefits; check with Lee County EDO.
- **Timing:**
  - Current (2018-round) QOZ designations **expire 12/31/2026**.
  - New 2027 QOZs run 1/1/2027–12/31/2036, under Rev. Proc. 2026-14. States nominate tracts, and per the Rev. Proc. Treasury certifies them by late 2026 (determination period as late as 10/28/2026, and certification up to 11/27/2026, or 12/28/2026 with an extension).
  - The Lee tracts may or may not be re-designated. **Check Florida's 2027 nomination list before buying property in a current tract for a post-2026 investment.**
  - Some Lee tracts may qualify as "rural" for the 30% step-up under the Treasury methodology. Most of the Cape Coral/Fort Myers urbanized area likely would not.

## 6) Key risks and gotchas
- **Failing the 90% test:** The QOF owes a **monthly penalty** equal to the shortfall (90% of assets minus the QOZP held) times the §6621(a)(2) underpayment rate. In a partnership the penalty is passed through to partners. There is a **reasonable-cause** exception (§1400Z-2(f)). Repeated or serious failure can lead to **decertification**, which ends the benefits and can trigger inclusion of the deferred gain (Regs §1.1400Z2(f)-1).
- **Inclusion events:** Selling, gifting or distributing the QOF interest early triggers the deferred gain. So can certain distributions that exceed basis, or debt-financed distributions within 2 years.
- **Related-party rules:** Property must be bought from an unrelated party, applying §267(b) and §707(b) with a **20%** threshold in place of 50% (§1400Z-2(e)(2)). Contributing land you already own does not create QOZBP purchased by the fund. Selling the gain asset to a related party also disqualifies the gain.
- **Working-capital safe harbor pitfalls:**
  - The written plan and schedule are missing or vague.
  - Funds are not spent on schedule.
  - The plan fails to tie the cash to QOZ property.
  - The safe harbor is used at the QOF level, where it does not apply.
- **Substantial improvement:** The building basis has to be doubled within 30 months. Land costs don't count toward the improvement, and buying a raw lot to hold idle risks failing the anti-abuse rules.
- **Sin-business and active-business rules:** Triple-net or passive leases, and leases to excluded businesses, can disqualify a QOZB. An auto dealership is not on the excluded list.
- **Cash timing:** Invest within 180 days. Cash received in the 6 months before a testing date can be excluded, but idle cash later counts against the 90% test.
- **Legacy deadline cliff:** For pre-2027 investments the deferred gain is due 12/31/2026 regardless, so investing in late 2026 gives almost no deferral. The 10-year exclusion still matters, but the zone itself expires. Investments in a tract that has expired after 2026 can still qualify for legacy funds under the regs (holding period through 2047), though new investments in that tract cannot.
- **Paperwork:** Form 8996 must be filed every year. Investors file Form 8997 every year. Records must be kept for the 10-year hold or more.
